# User test and interview, 2026-10-24

The first time someone who writes grants for a living used GrantBridge, and a structured interview with them
afterwards.

## The session

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Participant** | A grant researcher and writer at Austin's Second Chance, a small nonprofit whose community outreach works with at-risk adolescents and young men, providing stabilization through community services and engagement in the humanities. |
| **Date** | 2026-10-24 |
| **Duration** | 20 to 30 minutes for the hands-on session, plus a follow-up interview the same day |
| **Format** | Unmoderated. The participant worked through the product on their own, then answered a written interview. Both reached this repository through the project author. |
| **Tasks** | Set up an organization profile; search Grants.gov for a real opportunity; produce an outline for a specific one. |
| **Outcome** | **All tasks completed unaided.** |
| **Consent** | Given, for the session to be described and for the quotation below to be published, attributed as the participant chose. |
| **Recording** | None. No audio, video, screen recording or transcript. One task was timed with a stopwatch (below); nothing else was. |

## Their organization, in their words

- Role: **grant researcher and writer**; applications are worked on by a team, including them.
- Volume: **6 to 12 grant applications a year**.
- How a solicitation is handled today: *read, annotate and disseminate amongst interior parties.*

## The "before": what this costs them today

These are the participant's **estimates from memory**, not measurements. Nothing in this repository was timed
on their machine.

| Question | Their answer |
| --- | --- |
| From opening a federal NOFO to having a usable list of requirements and sections | **"Generally, several hours to read and prep an outline."** |
| Share of effort spent reading and organizing versus writing | **"Several hours at a minimum across multiple parties"**, depending on the grant |
| Has a requirement, form or deadline ever been missed because it was buried in a solicitation? | **"Yes, multiple times."** |

The third answer is the one that matters most for this project: the failure GrantBridge's outline is built to
prevent is one this organization has actually had.

## The "after": what they saw

- On the outlines GrantBridge produced: *"The outlines were detailed and appeared to have concise summaries of
  required information."* They reported **no errors or omissions** in them.
- On trusting the output: they would **re-read the solicitation and compare notes at first**, and rely on the
  outline only *"assuming there are no oversights after a few iterations."* That is the right posture, and it
  is the one GrantBridge's interface asks for: every item is shown beside the sentence it came from so it can
  be checked rather than trusted.
### The timed run

With the participant present on 2026-10-24, one task was timed end to end: **from the solicitation to a usable
outline took 45 seconds.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| What was timed | Taking a solicitation through GrantBridge until a usable outline was on screen |
| **Measured** | **45 seconds** |
| Their estimate for the same result by hand | *"Generally, several hours to read and prep an outline"* |
| Timed by | The project author, with the participant, on the participant's chosen document |
| Not recorded | Which solicitation, and its length. A second run was not done, so this is one observation, not an average |

**What the 45 seconds does not include.** It is the time to produce the outline, not the time to trust it. The
same participant said they would *"reread and compare notes"* at first and rely on the outline only *"assuming
there are no oversights after a few iterations"*. Their several-hour estimate also covers annotating and
disseminating the result among colleagues, which GrantBridge does not do. **The honest reading is that the
reading-and-structuring step went from a recalled several hours to a measured 45 seconds; it is not a claim
that a grant application now takes 45 seconds, and it is not a saved-time figure.**

For comparison, measured in this repository rather than on their machine: reading a real federal notice into
an outline took **19.8 to 26.8 seconds and cost about $0.003**, across the three notices in
`docs/evidence/outline-import-cost-2026-10-25.md`. The difference between that and the 45 seconds is a person
uploading a file and reading the result.

## Would it change anything

> *"Absolutely — there are tons of grants that seem great on paper but are hampered by implementation
> requirements and inadequate scope/oversight details we'd otherwise start and have to scratch, so this helps
> expedite that determination."*

This is the project's central claim — decide before you write — described independently by someone who does the
work. Asked what would stop them adopting it: *"As it stands, there are few things that would stop us from
utilizing this resource in its current iteration."*

## Why the Word round trip matters

Asked whether they would write in the browser or in Word:

> *"We would never write in a browser, as information would be shared across our writers, researchers,
> medical/psych professionals, and director board before being submitted."*

The Word download and upload was built because this participant asked for it. Their answer explains why a
browser-only drafting surface would have made the product unusable for their organization, whatever else it
did well.

## The quotation

Given for publication, attributed as the participant asked: **"a nonprofit grant developer for Austin's Second
Chance."**

> *"This is, objectively, a time saver at its worst. At best, if utilized to its fullest, we'd save dozens of
> hours per proposal/draft by having everything prepped in advance and, in this case, with the
> oversight/analytics provided by the site, an extra set of eyes checking for requirements and suitability of
> our proposal would help streamline submissions and expedite services provided."*

**"Dozens of hours per proposal" is the participant's own judgement of a potential benefit. It is not a
measurement, GrantBridge has not demonstrated it, and this repository does not repeat it as a finding.**

## The seven observations, and what changed

Two were defects. Neither was reachable from the project's sample data: both needed a live Grants.gov search
against a real state.

| # | Observation (as relayed) | Kind | What changed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The password length seems a bit excessive, requiring a 12-character password | Usability | Minimum lowered from 12 to 10. NIST SP 800-63B asks for 8 or more with no composition rules, so 10 keeps a margin. Argon2id hashing, the five-failure lockout and session expiry are unchanged. |
| 2 | One grant showed as forecasted but had a closing date of 2019 on Grants.gov | **Defect** | `live_deadline()` checked for a past close date on the `posted` and `listed` branches but not on `forecasted`, so a stale forecast read as "not accepting applications yet". It now reads "Forecast, estimated close date passed" and is hidden with other closed grants. |
| 3 | It pulled a U.S. embassy in Cairo grant twice when searching for grants in Virginia | **Defect** | There was no deduplication anywhere in the search path. Results now merge on opportunity number, keeping the better-dated record so a stale forecast cannot hide a live posting, and the page reports how many merged. |
| 4 | A sliding funding request range on Find Grants would let a team pursue a broader range | Feature | An award-size range on Find Grants, applied to both sources. Grants.gov cannot filter by award size, so GrantBridge applies it to the results. A grant whose size the funder never stated is kept. |
| 5 | The site mentions Connecticut a lot, which would confuse users in other states | Usability | Removed from five places in the interface; the pages offering demo data now say the sample organization's state is only an example and checks run against the user's own. |
| 6 | The "Read the documents" button is confusing | Usability | Renamed **Analyze the solicitation**. |
| 7 | Let the applicant export the outline to Word, work there, and upload it back | Feature | **Work in Word instead**: the outline downloads as a .docx in the funder's order with each section's requirements in a boxed note, and uploads back with a change summary before anything saves. Requirements travel in Word tables so they can never return as the writer's prose; a renamed heading is reported, not guessed at. |

In the interview the participant confirmed the first defect was the only thing that seemed wrong:
*"the only thing that seemed amiss was that information for outdated grants was populated, but feedback was
provided to the developer that should correct the issue."* It has been corrected, and a test holds it.

## What locks each fix

| Fix | Test |
| --- | --- |
| Stale forecast | `tests/test_grant_search.py::test_a_forecast_whose_close_date_has_passed_is_not_called_upcoming`, plus three covering forecasts still to come, forecasts with no date, and hiding stale ones |
| Duplicates | `test_one_opportunity_listed_twice_is_merged`, `test_merging_keeps_the_better_dated_record`, `test_two_different_opportunities_are_both_kept` |
| Award range | `test_the_award_range_keeps_grants_whose_size_the_funder_never_stated`, `test_an_open_topped_range_has_no_ceiling`, `test_live_results_outside_the_range_are_hidden_and_counted` |
| Word round trip | `tests/test_outline_docx.py`, 13 cases: requirements never return as prose, a renamed heading is reported, emptying a section is counted, a non-.docx is refused by its bytes |
| Password length | `tests/test_auth.py`, run against the new minimum |

## Limits

- **One participant, one session.** Nothing here is a sample, a rate, or a statistic, and none of it supports a
  claim about users in general.
- **One task was timed; the baseline was not.** The 45 seconds is measured, once, on one document. The
  "several hours" it is set against is the participant's recollection, not a measurement, and no one timed
  them doing the work by hand. A single timed run is an observation, not an average.
- **"Dozens of hours" is their estimate of a potential benefit, not a result.**
- **Unmoderated and relayed.** The session findings and the interview answers both reached this repository
  through the project author. Everything in quotation marks is the participant's own words, quoted with their
  permission; they agreed that any of their answers could be quoted.
- The participant was recruited by the project author and is not independent of the project.
- The outline they reviewed was judged correct on sight. Nobody has yet checked a GrantBridge outline against a
  funder's own requirements line by line, or submitted an application prepared with it.
