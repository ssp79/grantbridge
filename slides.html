The problem

Small teams lose the most before they write a word

92%

of U.S. nonprofits spend less than $1 million a year.

45%

of nonprofits with government grants or contracts reported problems with late payments, in a national survey (2012 data)

  • Applications compete with programs for the same few staff hours.
  • The costly mistakes come before writing: an ineligible grant, colliding deadlines, mission drift.
  • And winning can cost cash first: a required match, and payment only after you spend.
  • Writing assistants start at the draft. GrantBridge starts at the decision.

Sources: National Council of Nonprofits, Nonprofit Impact Matters (2019 report); Urban Institute, Nonprofit-Government Contracts and Grants: Findings from the 2013 National Survey, p. 36. Both accessed 2026-10-21.

What is new

Everything before the writing, and everything after it

Discovery tools end where the hard part begins; writing assistants start after the expensive decision is already made. GrantBridge is the five steps in between — and code, not the model, makes every call that has to be right.

1 · Should we pursue it?

Eligibility, fit, readiness, staff hours and budget, each from a rule printed on the page. The AI adds evidence; it cannot override a check.

No fortnight spent on a grant you were never eligible for.

2 · Can we afford to win it? new

Quotes the match, the reimbursement delay and the overhead cap, then simulates the cash winning would tie up.

Catches the grant that fits but drains the bank account.

3 · Which ones, together?

Grants scheduled against the team's real weekly hours; mission drift flagged in the funder's own words.

A plan the team can actually staff.

4 · The funder's own outline new

The solicitation read into the sections it prescribes, what each must contain, the forms and the rubric.

The hour of copying that starts every application.

5 · Did we actually answer it? new

A requirement counts as addressed only when the review can quote the sentence in your answer that covers it.

Find the gap before the funder does.

Every item above carries the funder's own sentence, and unknown is never treated as a no. Seven competitors' own pages, read on 2026-10-21: several claim fit or review features. None described simulating one grant's cash needs against reserves, scheduling against staff hours, or mission-drift checks. Closest to the first: an organization-wide self-assessment (GrantStation) and post-award tracking (Instrumentl). Details: docs/COMPETITORS.md.

Safety

Rules over the model

  • Grant and notice text is data: injected instructions are flagged, never followed.
  • An award term needs a verbatim quote, or it stays unknown. GrantBridge never assumes a typical term.
  • Live red-team: every check passed (nothing leaked, nothing saved).
  • Every query is scoped to one account. Signed out, only About and Sign in exist.
  • A real notice broke the filter. A contact called Dan matched the DAN jailbreak name; that 3-letter match would have silently dropped any requirement containing guidance. Now matched on whole words.

Impact

A grant professional used it, and it found two real bugs

45 s

measured: solicitation to a usable outline

2

defects found that sample data could not expose

906

automated tests passed, 52 of 52 browser steps

A grant researcher and writer at Austin's Second Chance, whose team runs 6 to 12 applications a year, worked through it unaided and gave seven observations. All seven were fixed.

They put the same job by hand at "several hours to read and prep an outline" — a recollection, not a measurement. Asked whether a requirement had ever been missed because it was buried in a solicitation: "Yes, multiple times."

"There are tons of grants that seem great on paper but are hampered by implementation requirements and inadequate scope/oversight details we'd otherwise start and have to scratch, so this helps expedite that determination."

One participant, one unmoderated session, recruited by the author. The 45 seconds was timed once, on one document, and is the time to produce the outline, not to trust it. No claim is made about users in general.
